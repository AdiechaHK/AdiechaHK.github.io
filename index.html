About Me

I put AI systems into production and keep them there. A vision system deciding who gets through a secured door. A speech-to-text → LLM → speech loop that has to answer before a person notices the gap. An agent that assembles our engineering reports and cites its evidence. Not demos — things other people now depend on.

The reason I can be trusted with that is the thirteen years underneath it. I've owned a high-throughput platform that predated me by eight years and never stopped running, moved a live production database onto new infrastructure with ten seconds of downtime, and I'm currently architecting a ground-up rebuild of an enterprise B2B platform as Tech Lead at WebOccult Technologies.

Most of my work has been close to the people using it — four years embedded with a product group in Portugal, a deployment on a customer site in Japan, enterprise workflows I had to understand properly before I could model them. I think that's the interesting part of the job. I also speak at Laravel Rajkot and Laravel Ahmedabad meetups.

Selected Work

Face recognition driving a physical lock WebOccult · 2025

Led engineering for an access-control system on a customer site in Japan — an in-house vision API deciding who may pass through a secured door, and commercial lock hardware that had never heard of it. The model was the easy half. A vision model returns a confidence score and a lock needs a yes or no, and the yes is physical and irreversible. Where that threshold sits, and how it fails when the camera is dazzled or the network drops, is the actual system.

Ran daily in production, on a site I had no control over

Moving a live database without taking it down WebOccult · 2025

A production database had outgrown the host it shared with other services and started starving them of memory. Moved it to dedicated infrastructure via replica-set promotion with a scripted cutover: quiesce writers, verify sync, promote secondary, rotate credentials, restart.

Ten seconds of unplanned downtime, inside a single planned window

When an assumption underneath a system quietly stopped being true Improwised · 2019–2022

An eight-year-old platform was built on the premise that the identifiers it received from upstream were stable. They stopped being stable, so records that were one thing started arriving as several. Rather than rebuild, I added a content hash over the attributes that actually identify a record as a fallback key, checked only when the primary ID misses — fixing integrity without breaking existing API contracts.

Integrity restored without breaking a single published contract
