Ten directions

Same content throughout — the six systems, the capabilities, the history, the talks. What changes is the idea the page is built on. Every one is a standalone file: pick one and it becomes the root index.html.

The interactive one

Your idea. A different category from the rest — a page you use rather than scroll.

Ask HK

Most memorable

The site is a chat. Command chips (/work, /experience, /stack…) type themselves into the input and send, then the answer streams back — prose, stat tiles, work cards. Visitors can also just type a question: why Go, what was the hardest problem, tell me about Japan. Each answer offers follow-ups.

Why it works: you're positioning as someone who builds AI systems, and the page is one. It's honest about being pattern-matched notes rather than a live model — which is itself the kind of judgement the job is about.

My three picks

Built from a concept rather than a layout. These are the ones I'd argue for.

Still Running

My favourite

The page is a status page. It opens with a green dot and "All systems operational", and every system you've built is a service with a status badge and an uptime bar. Calm, spacious, almost no decoration — the confidence comes from the format, not from effects.

Why: your best stories are all about things that didn't break. This makes that the entire argument, and it's the one page here a hiring manager would remember by its idea rather than its colours.

Fieldwork

Best for FDE

Warm paper, serif headlines, a "sites worked" panel — Japan, Portugal, Rajkot, Ahmedabad — and each system written up as a field report: Situation / What I did / Outcome, with a rotated stamp in the margin.

Why: it's built on the thing that actually makes you an FDE candidate — that the work happened in other people's buildings. No other page here says that, and this one says nothing else.

One Paragraph

Most confident

Your whole career as a single large paragraph. Six phrases in it are underlined; click one and the system behind it opens below. No hero, no cards, no grid — just the sentence and what's underneath it.

Why: it takes nerve, and it only works if the writing is good. Yours is. The risk is that a recruiter skimming for keywords finds a wall of prose.

With a signature device

Closer to the two sites you sent me — each carries one memorable visual idea.

Ambient

Closest to your refs

Dark and atmospheric — a drifting field of your own vocabulary (Japan, SQS, 36GB, Grafana, replica set) behind a big glowing name, a terminal chip that types your roles, count-up stats, fixed social rail. The Nainesh energy, in amber rather than borrowed red.

Device: the ambient keyword field.

Blueprint

The page is an engineering drawing. Graph-paper ground, a bordered sheet with corner nodes, a title block with revision number, a spec strip, dimension lines, each system as FIG. 01–06. History becomes a revision table.

Device: technical drafting. Quietly argues you're an architect.

Signal

An animated pipeline runs through the hero — mic → speech-to-text → LLM → speech, with the signal flowing along it. Every work card carries its own small flow diagram.

Device: data flow made visible. Thematically yours — you build pipelines.

Quieter, typographic

The first batch. Restrained and safe; the content does all the work.

Case study

Bold opening claim, four hard numbers under it, each system as problem → approach → result. Puts the evidence where a hiring manager skims.

Strongest for: applications and recruiters deciding in ninety seconds.

Editorial

Serif, single column, generous measure. Work entries become short pieces of writing about decisions rather than bullet lists.

Strongest for: signalling that you think and communicate clearly.

Systems

Monospace throughout, labelled records, a metric band, log-style history. Highest information density of the nine.

Strongest for: engineering audiences — though it's the most common genre, so the least differentiating.

Theme Lab

Tool

Not a design — a live editor for the chat theme. Colour pickers for every token on the left, the real page on the right updating as you drag. Six starting palettes, WCAG contrast readout, and a Copy CSS button that gives you the block to paste back.

If one is close but not right, say which and what's off. Mixing is easy — Fieldwork's field-report structure inside Still Running's status framing would work, for instance.
